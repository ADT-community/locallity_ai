Save and Grow is an ecosystem approach to farming that aims to produce more from the same land while conserving natural resources. For maize, rice and wheat, the guide recommends three mutually reinforcing practices: minimum soil disturbance, permanent soil cover with crop residues or cover crops, and diversification through rotations and associations. Together these practices rebuild soil organic matter, improve water infiltration and storage, reduce erosion, and lower labour costs over time.

For maize-based systems, rotating with grain legumes supplies nitrogen and breaks pest and disease cycles. Residues should be retained on the field instead of burned, and planted directly through the mulch where possible. In rice, alternate wetting and drying can save water without reducing yield when it is managed correctly, and integrating rice with fish or ducks can add income and help control pests. Wheat systems benefit from diversifying rotations and from using varieties suited to local heat and drought conditions.

The guide stresses that intensification must be adapted to local conditions. Good seed of adapted varieties, precise placement of modest amounts of fertilizer, timely weeding, and attention to planting dates are as important as the conservation practices themselves. Compost, manure and legume residues can reduce the need for purchased inputs, but nutrient removal in harvested grain must be replaced to avoid mining the soil.

Farmers are encouraged to experiment on small plots, observe results, and adapt practices with local extension services. The approach is especially relevant to smallholders facing rising input costs and more erratic rainfall, because it lowers risk, protects the soil, and can raise yields sustainably without depending on large purchases of agrochemicals.
