Maize is the most important food crop in Tanzania, grown mostly under rain-fed, low-input conditions. Good agricultural practices start with choosing a variety suited to the local agro-ecology and to the length of the rainy season. Test germination before planting and buy seed from a recognized source. Plant at the start of the reliable rains, at the recommended spacing for a sole crop, and thin or fill gaps early so that the final plant population is even. Intercropping or rotating maize with grain legumes such as cowpea, beans or groundnut helps restore soil fertility and gives extra food and income.

Check soil fertility before the season, using a simple visual assessment or a soil test where available. Apply compost or animal manure, and use mineral fertilizer at the recommended rate, split between planting and top-dressing, so that nutrients are available when the crop needs them. Where soils are acid, agricultural lime may be needed. Weed early, because the first weeks after emergence are the most sensitive to competition.

Scout fields regularly for maize stem borer, fall armyworm, termites and disease symptoms such as maize lethal necrosis. For fall armyworm, look for fresh whorl damage and larvae, and use integrated options: resistant or tolerant varieties, timely planting, intercropping, hand-picking where feasible, and approved biopesticides. Avoid spraying indiscriminately, since this kills natural enemies and can be unsafe for the applicator.

Harvest when grains are fully mature and dry the crop thoroughly before shelling. Store shelled grain in a clean, dry, well-ventilated store or sealed containers, keep it off the floor, and check regularly for insects and mould. Good drying and storage are the cheapest way to protect the harvest and the family's food supply.
