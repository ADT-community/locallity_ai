This technical report from the Technologies for African Agricultural Transformation programme reviews climate-smart agricultural technologies for the Sahel and the Horn of Africa. The region faces low and erratic rainfall, high temperatures, degraded soils and frequent droughts. The report collects practical options that help farmers produce more while adapting to climate change and, where possible, reducing emissions.

The technologies include drought-tolerant and early-maturing varieties of sorghum, millet, maize and groundnut; soil and water conservation measures such as tied ridges, zai pits and mulching; small-scale irrigation and water harvesting; and integrated soil fertility management that combines manure, compost and modest amounts of mineral fertilizer. Micro-dosing, or placing a small quantity of fertilizer close to the seed, is highlighted as a way to raise yields with limited cash and thin soils.

Intercropping cereals with legumes such as cowpea and groundnut improves soil fertility, diversifies the diet and reduces the risk of total crop failure. Groundnut, for example, grows well in rotation with cereals on lighter soils and needs calcium as well as phosphorus and potassium for pod development. Agroforestry and the use of crop residues help restore organic matter and protect fields from wind and erosion.

The report stresses that no single technology works everywhere. Farmers should choose options that fit their rainfall zone, soils, labour and access to markets, and should test them on a small plot before adopting them widely. National extension services and research institutes are the right source of locally adapted varieties and input recommendations.
