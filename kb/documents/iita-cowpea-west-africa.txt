Cowpea is a key grain legume in West Africa, providing protein-rich food, fodder and income. Yields are often low because of insect pests, diseases, the parasitic weed Striga, poor soils and intermittent drought. The guide, prepared by IITA and national partners for Nigeria, Mali, Burkina Faso, Niger, Ghana and Senegal, sets out the steps farmers can take to improve production.

Select a well-drained sandy loam for a rain-fed crop, since cowpea does not tolerate waterlogging. Clear the land, prepare a fine seedbed, and use good-quality seed without damage holes or wrinkles. The planting date matters: photoperiod-sensitive varieties should be planted from mid-July to mid-August in West Africa so that flowering is not delayed by long days, while early, photoperiod-insensitive varieties can be planted whenever rainfall is sufficient. Choose a variety suited to the zone, considering maturity, drought tolerance, Striga resistance and consumer preference for seed colour and size.

Sow at the recommended spacing for sole crops, or about 75 cm between rows and 25 cm within rows for a cowpea-maize intercrop; plant cowpea about four to six weeks after the cereal. Apply fertilizer according to the guide and manage weeds early. The major constraints include fungal diseases, bacterial blight, viruses, pod-sucking bugs, flower thrips and storage weevils. Resistant varieties, timely planting, good field hygiene and carefully timed, approved spraying where necessary are all part of management.

Harvest pods when they are mature but before they split in the field, dry the grain well, and store it in clean, dry containers to protect it from storage pests.
