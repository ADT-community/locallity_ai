Cowpea is widely grown in northern Nigeria, where it supplies food and fodder in the dry savannas. This IITA guide describes the practical steps for establishing and managing the crop under the conditions of the region. As in the rest of West Africa, success depends on using adapted varieties, planting at the right time and controlling the major pests and diseases.

Choose a well-drained sandy or sandy-loam soil, because cowpea is sensitive to waterlogging. Clear and prepare the land early, and make ridges where this helps drainage or weed control. Select clean, healthy seed of a variety recommended for northern Nigeria, taking into account maturity period, drought tolerance, resistance to Striga and diseases, and the market preference for seed size and colour. Treat seed before sowing where recommended.

Planting date affects both yield and the need for insect control. Where rainfall is limited, sow so that flowering and pod filling occur while moisture is still available. In the northern savannas, later planting can help avoid the heaviest pest pressure, but it must not expose the crop to drought at flowering. Sow at the recommended intra-row and inter-row spacing and depth; for intercrops with cereals, follow the arrangements given in the guide so that the cowpea receives enough light.

Apply the recommended fertilizer, and weed during the early weeks. The main insect problems are thrips before flowering, pod-sucking bugs during pod formation, and storage weevils after harvest. Diseases include fungal leaf spots, bacterial blight and viruses. Use resistant varieties, clean seed, field hygiene and approved control measures where needed. Harvest pods as they ripen, dry the seed thoroughly, and store it in clean, dry conditions to protect it from weevils and mould.
