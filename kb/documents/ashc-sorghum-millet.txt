Sorghum and millet are the staple cereals of many dry areas of Africa. They grow where maize often fails: millet is the most drought tolerant, then sorghum. This cropping guide, produced by the Africa Soil Health Consortium, shows extension workers and farmers how to combine these cereals with legumes such as cowpea, groundnut and soybean in intercrops or rotations.

Average yields of sorghum and millet in Africa are only about one and 0.5 tonnes per hectare, but they can often be doubled with good seed and good management. Use improved varieties, plant on time at the recommended spacing, weed early, and apply organic matter and mineral fertilizer where possible. Soils in sorghum and millet areas are often poor and fallows are short, so integrated soil fertility management is essential. Sorghum responds better to improved soil conditions than millet.

Intercropping with legumes improves yields, soil fertility and food security. Cowpea and groundnut are the most common partners; soybean is suited only to wetter zones with more than about 700 mm of rain per year. A cereal-legume intercrop should be planted in rows that make harvesting and weeding possible, and the legume should be given enough light. Rotations with legumes help control Striga and other pests.

In the drier environments, minimum tillage and retention of crop residues conserve scarce moisture and reduce erosion. Plant with the first reliable rains and thin seedlings to the correct population. Harvest the panicles when grain is mature, dry them thoroughly and store the grain in dry, clean conditions to prevent mould and insect damage. Local extension services should be consulted for the varieties and fertilizer rates recommended in each district.
