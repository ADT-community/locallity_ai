Growing cassava in Nigeria is a practical IITA manual for farmers and extension workers. Cassava is one of the most important staple crops in Nigeria and much of West Africa, and it can be grown on a small scale with limited inputs. This guide describes the basic steps needed to obtain a good harvest.

Start with healthy planting material. Use stem cuttings from vigorous plants that show no symptoms of mosaic disease or other problems, and take the cuttings from a healthy stem of the right age. Prepare the land properly: clear weeds, make ridges or mounds where the soil is heavy or shallow, and plant at the recommended spacing so that plants do not compete excessively. Plant at the beginning of the rainy season so that the crop establishes with adequate moisture.

Weed the field during the early months, since cassava is sensitive to weed competition before the canopy closes. Avoid damaging the stems during weeding. Where practical, intercrop with a short-duration legume such as cowpea to get an early harvest and help suppress weeds. Watch for cassava mosaic disease and cassava brown streak disease; remove infected plants and always use clean planting material, since these diseases are spread through stems and by whiteflies.

Cassava roots are normally harvested several months after planting, depending on the variety. Dig them up carefully to avoid cuts and bruises, because damaged roots rot quickly. Fresh roots do not store for long, so either take them to market promptly or process them into gari, flour, chips or other products. Dry products store much longer and can be sold when prices are better, giving farmers more control over income.
