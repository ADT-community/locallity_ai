This rice production manual, prepared by IITA and the Tanzania Agricultural Research Institute, is written for smallholder rice farmers and extension workers in Tanzania. It covers the whole crop cycle in nine modules: choosing a good variety, land preparation, seed preparation, planting, field management, the cropping calendar, and managing salt-affected, saline-sodic and calcareous soils.

Start with a variety that is suited to the local environment and to farmers' preferences for taste and market price. Prepare the field by clearing, tilling and levelling it, and make bunds to hold water. Level land is important because it allows an even water depth, which improves weed control and makes harvesting easier. Select good seed, and consider pre-sprouting or priming seed to improve germination and establish the crop quickly.

Rice can be established by direct sowing or by transplanting seedlings. Transplant at the right age and spacing, with the correct number of seedlings per hill. Water management is critical: keep the field flooded during establishment, but use alternate wetting and drying where appropriate to save water. The manual describes the field water tube, a simple device that tells the farmer when the water level has fallen far enough to re-irrigate. Weed early, since weeds compete strongly with young rice.

A cropping calendar helps plan land preparation, planting, weeding, fertilizer application and harvest so that farm operations are done on time. On problem soils, use the specific recommendations in the manual: improved drainage and amendments for saline or sodic soils, and appropriate nutrient management for calcareous soils. Dry the grain thoroughly before storage to avoid mould and loss.
