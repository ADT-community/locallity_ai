This IITA training manual gives an overview of cowpea production, from field selection to storage. Cowpea is grown across the tropics for its grain, leaves and fodder, and it fits well into rotations and intercrops because it fixes nitrogen. The manual is designed for training courses and extension work, so each stage is described in practical terms.

Good seed is the first requirement. Use seed that is clean, well stored and free from insect damage, and test germination before sowing. Select a well-drained site: cowpea dislikes heavy, waterlogged soils. Prepare the land so that the taproot can penetrate, and sow at the correct depth and spacing. Planting date should be chosen so that flowering and pod filling avoid the worst drought and, where photoperiod-sensitive varieties are used, so that flowering is not delayed by long days.

Fertilizer requirements are modest but phosphorus is often needed; apply it at planting where soils are deficient. Keep the crop weed-free early, because cowpea is a poor competitor in its first weeks. The major insect pests attack flowers and pods, and several fungal, bacterial and viral diseases can reduce yield. Resistant varieties, timely planting, crop rotation and good field hygiene are the foundation of control. Where pesticides are justified, use only approved products at the correct dose and observe the interval before harvesting or grazing.

Harvest when pods are mature, and avoid leaving them too long in the field where they may split or be attacked by insects. Dry the grain to a safe moisture level, thresh carefully, and store in clean, dry, sealed containers. Storage pests can destroy a harvest that was otherwise successful, so check stored grain regularly and keep stores dry and well ventilated.
