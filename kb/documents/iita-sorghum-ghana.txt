This IITA guide, produced with Ghana's Savannah Agricultural Research Institute, gives practical advice for sustainable sorghum production in Ghana. Sorghum is a drought-tolerant cereal grown mainly in the savannas, where it is used for food, feed and brewing. Yields are usually low because farmers use traditional varieties, plant late, weed late and apply few nutrients.

Choose a variety recommended for the agro-ecological zone, based on maturity, yield potential, resistance to pests and diseases, tolerance of Striga and drought, and market or household preferences. Use good-quality seed and treat it where recommended. Prepare the land early and plant with the onset of the reliable rains, at the correct depth and spacing. Early planting lets the crop use the available moisture and escape the worst pest pressure.

Fertility management combines organic and mineral sources. Apply compost or manure, and use fertilizer at the recommended rate, placing it near the plants. Where soils are acid, lime may be needed. Weed during the first weeks after emergence; late weeding causes large yield losses. Sorghum can be intercropped or rotated with legumes such as cowpea, soybean or groundnut to improve soil fertility and diversify the harvest.

Important pests and diseases include stem borers, aphids, grain mould, leaf diseases and the parasitic weed Striga. Use resistant varieties, good crop hygiene and approved control measures where necessary. Harvest when the grain is mature, dry the panicles thoroughly, and store the threshed grain in clean, dry containers. Consult local extension officers for the varieties and recommendations appropriate to a specific district, since conditions differ across Ghana's sorghum-growing areas.
