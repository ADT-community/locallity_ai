This FAO technical guide describes conservation agriculture and sustainable intensification practices for small-scale farmers in Zambia. It focuses on maize-based systems, often combined with grain legumes such as pigeon pea, and on the services and markets farmers need to adopt these practices.

The core principles are minimum soil disturbance, maintaining a permanent soil cover and diversifying crop species. Instead of ploughing every season, farmers open small planting basins or use direct seeding, leaving crop residues on the surface. The residues protect the soil from erosion, reduce evaporation and gradually build organic matter. Over time, this improves soil structure and water infiltration, which matters in seasons with erratic rainfall.

Rotations and associations with legumes, especially pigeon pea, supply nitrogen and give farmers an additional food and income source. The guide gives practical details on choosing adapted seed, deciding planting dates and spacing, managing weeds without a plough, and applying fertilizer precisely where it is needed. Fire should be kept off the field, since burning residues destroys the soil cover.

Beyond the field, the guide discusses mechanization services, better links to agri-business hubs and access to markets. Smallholders often lack equipment and credit, so shared services and farmer organizations can make conservation farming practical. Farmers are encouraged to start on a small area, compare yields and labour with their usual practice, and adapt the system with local extension advice before expanding it to the whole farm.
