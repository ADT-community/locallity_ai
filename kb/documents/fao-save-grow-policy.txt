This FAO policymaker's guide explains why smallholder crop production must be intensified in an environmentally sustainable way. Food demand is rising, but nearly all additional production in the future must come from existing farmland, much of it already degraded. Continuing to expand into forests and marginal land is not a viable option.

The guide sets out the principles of sustainable intensification: produce more from the same area while enhancing natural capital and ecosystem services. In practice this means combining better varieties, water management, soil fertility management and pest management, and reducing waste along the value chain. Conservation agriculture — minimum tillage, permanent soil cover and crop diversification — is one important component, but it must be adapted to local farming systems rather than applied as a fixed package.

Smallholders need support to intensify: access to good seed, fertilizer and equipment, credit, extension advice, reliable markets and secure land rights. Women and young people are often the main contributors to farm labour and need to be included in these services. The guide also emphasizes that higher yields alone do not guarantee better nutrition or incomes; diversification, value addition and market access matter too.

For the purpose of this knowledge base, the relevant message is that advice to farmers should promote practices that raise productivity without exhausting the soil, and should be adapted to local conditions through farmer participation and good extension services. General recommendations from this guide should always be combined with locally specific advice on varieties, planting dates and input rates.
