This CIMMYT farmer's manual describes how to grow maize, sorghum and millet in rotation or intercrop with legumes such as common beans, soybeans, groundnuts and cowpea in eastern and southern Africa. These cereals are the staple food of the region and the main source of dietary carbohydrate, but continuous sole cropping on the same land depletes soils and increases pest and disease problems.

Rotations and intercrops increase productivity and improve food and nutrition security. A legume following a cereal leaves nitrogen in the soil and breaks the cycle of pests such as Striga and stem borers. An intercrop spreads the risk of crop failure: if the cereal suffers from drought, the legume may still yield, and vice versa. The manual gives step-by-step guidance on choosing the species and variety, arranging rows, planting at the right time and density, and applying fertilizer and manure.

Timeliness is repeatedly emphasized. Plant with the first effective rains, weed early, and top-dress when the crop is actively growing. In drier areas, reduced tillage and retention of residues conserve moisture. Where labour is limited, a relay crop planted into a standing cereal can make better use of the season.

The guide draws on experiences from Ethiopia, Malawi and South Africa and on wider work in sub-Saharan Africa. It stresses that the recommendations are general: farmers should adapt them to their own agro-ecology and consult local extension services for variety and input advice specific to their district. Keeping records of yields and costs helps farmers judge which intercrop or rotation works best on their own land.
