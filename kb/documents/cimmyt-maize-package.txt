This CIMMYT field manual sets out a package of practices for profitable maize cultivation. Maize can grow on many soil types, from loamy sand to heavy clay, but it performs best on well-drained soils with good organic matter, adequate water-holding capacity and near-neutral pH. Salt-affected soils should be avoided, and maize is very sensitive to waterlogging, so low-lying fields with poor drainage are unsuitable. Prolonged cold below about five degrees Celsius also harms the crop.

Begin with good seed of a suitable variety and a well-prepared seedbed. Plant at the recommended time and spacing so that the stand is uniform, and fill gaps or thin excess plants early. Apply organic manure or compost, together with mineral fertilizer where affordable, placing nutrients near the root zone so that the young crop can use them efficiently. Split nitrogen applications where possible to reduce losses.

Keep the field free of weeds, especially during the first weeks, and watch for major insect pests such as stem borers, termites and fall armyworm. Scout regularly and intervene early using approved, integrated control options. Avoid unnecessary insecticide use, which can harm beneficial insects and contaminate the crop.

Harvest when the grain is mature, then dry it to a safe moisture content before shelling. Store grain in a clean, dry, ventilated structure, protect it from insects and rodents, and inspect it regularly. The manual emphasizes that yield and profit come from combining several modest improvements — good seed, timely operations, balanced nutrients, pest control and safe storage — rather than from any single input.
