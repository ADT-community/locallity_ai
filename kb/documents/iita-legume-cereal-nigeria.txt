This farmers' guide from IITA explains how to raise the productivity of legume-cereal cropping systems in the savannas of Nigeria. Rotations and intercrops of cowpea, soybean or groundnut with maize, sorghum or millet make better use of land and labour, improve soil fertility through nitrogen fixation, and reduce the build-up of pests and diseases that occur when the same crop is grown every year.

Start by choosing the right combination for the farm. Where land is scarce, an intercrop gives two harvests from the same field; where land is more available, a rotation may be easier to manage. Use improved varieties of both crops that are suited to the local rainfall and soils. Prepare the land well and sow on time, since a delay of even a week can reduce yield significantly. Follow the recommended row arrangements, plant populations and fertilizer rates for each crop, and apply phosphorus where soils are deficient.

Weeding must be timely, especially during the first six weeks. The legume often covers the ground and suppresses later weeds, which reduces labour. Watch for major pests and diseases of both crops, including Striga, stem borers, foliar diseases and pod-sucking bugs. Where an intercrop is used, remember that spraying for one crop can affect the other, so choose products and timing carefully and follow all safety instructions.

After harvest, return crop residues to the field where possible to build organic matter. Rotate the cereals with a legume at least every two or three seasons. Keep simple records of the yield of each component and of inputs used, so that the farmer can compare systems and decide which one gives the best return. The guide is intended for extension workers and farmers who want practical, locally tested options rather than a single fixed recommendation.
