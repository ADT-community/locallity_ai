This WHO fact sheet summarizes why safe drinking water matters. Contaminated water can transmit diseases such as diarrhoea, cholera, dysentery, typhoid and polio, and unsafe water is responsible for a large number of diarrhoeal deaths each year. Access to a safely managed drinking-water service — water that is available, accessible and free from contamination — remains a major global challenge, including in the African Region.

Households can reduce risk by protecting the source, treating water where its safety is uncertain and storing it safely. Storage containers should be covered to keep out dust, insects and faecal contamination, and clean water should be drawn with a clean vessel or tap. Standing water in and around the home can provide breeding sites for mosquitoes that transmit malaria, dengue and other diseases, so containers should be emptied, cleaned or covered.

Water scarcity affects many communities and is worsening with population growth, urbanization andchanging rainfall patterns. When water is scarce, households may store more water in the home, which increases the risk of contamination if containers are not cleaned and covered. Water safety plans, used by utilities and communities, identify risks from the catchment to the tap and help prevent contamination before it happens.

WHO monitors drinking-water quality and supports countries in testing household water treatment technologies against health-based performance criteria. People who suspect that their water is unsafe, or who have symptoms such as persistent diarrhoea, should contact their local health provider or public health authority for advice specific to their situation.
