Household water treatment and safe storage (HWTS) improve the quality of drinking water and reduce diarrhoeal disease, especially for people who rely on unimproved water sources or on supplies that are not always safe. This is particularly important in emergencies, when normal water services may be disrupted.

WHO explains that treatment is only one part of the solution. Water that has been treated can easily become contaminated again during transport and storage, so the treated water must be kept in a clean, covered container, and water should be drawn with a clean cup or tap rather than by dipping hands or dirty vessels into it. Storage containers should have a narrow mouth or a tap, and they should be cleaned regularly.

Diarrhoeal disease can be caused by bacteria, viruses and parasites in water contaminated by faeces. Treatment methods that remove or inactivate these pathogens include boiling, chlorination, filtration and solar disinfection, depending on local conditions and on the quality of the source water. The effectiveness of a method depends on correct and consistent use, so households need clear instructions and reliable access to the product or fuel required. Covering water storage containers also reduces mosquito breeding, which can lower the risk of diseases such as dengue and malaria.

HWTS is most useful where the safety of the water supply is uncertain and where people are vulnerable, including young children and people with weakened immunity. It does not replace the need for safely managed water services, but it is a practical additional barrier against disease. For advice about a specific illness or outbreak, contact a local health professional or public health authority.
