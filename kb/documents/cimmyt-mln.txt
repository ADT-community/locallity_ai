Maize lethal necrosis is a serious viral disease of maize that has caused severe losses in East Africa. This CIMMYT technical manual brings together best practices for managing it, for countries where the disease is established and for high-risk countries that want to prevent its introduction. The disease results from the combination of two viruses, maize chlorotic mottle virus and a potyvirus such as sugarcane mosaic virus, and infected plants show mosaic patterns, stunting and rapid drying.

Because the viruses are transmitted by insects and can also be spread through seed and contaminated equipment, management starts with healthy seed and clean field practices. The manual describes surveillance and sampling protocols, leaf and seed sampling methods, and diagnostic tests for the viruses. Quarantine and phytosanitary measures help prevent movement of infected material into new areas.

Resistant or tolerant germplasm is the most durable control. The manual explains how varieties are screened under artificial inoculation at the Naivasha screening facility in Kenya, and how breeders select for resistance. For farmers, the practical advice is to obtain seed from reliable sources, avoid saving seed from diseased fields, remove and destroy infected plants early, and control insect vectors where recommended.

Early warning and emergency plans are described for national programmes. Farmers and extension workers should report unusual symptoms quickly so that outbreaks can be confirmed and contained. The manual also covers pathogen-free commercial seed production and standard operating procedures, because the seed system is a key part of preventing further spread. Local extension services should be consulted for the varieties currently recommended in a given area.
